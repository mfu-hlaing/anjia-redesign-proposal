The premise

Listings are a commodity. Confidence is not.

The harder questions arrive afterwards — whether the chanote is clean, whether the building's foreign quota has room left for your name, how the money legally enters the country, and who answers the telephone when the air conditioning fails in March and you are in Shanghai. That is the work. The photographs are only the invitation.

Ownership

Three lawful routes.
Most agents explain none of them.

A foreigner may not hold Thai land outright — but that is where the explanation usually stops, and it is not where the subject ends.

Most common

Freehold condominium

The unit is yours outright, in your own name, in perpetuity. The constraint is the quota: no more than 49% of a building's saleable area may be held by foreigners. When a building sits at quota you wait for a foreign seller to exit — or you take another route.

Interest
Outright
Term
Perpetual
Constraint
49% quota

Land & villas

Registered leasehold

A thirty-year lease registered at the Land Office, ordinarily written with renewal options. This is how most foreigners hold a villa, or the land beneath a house. Renewals are contractual rather than guaranteed — which is precisely why the drafting matters more than the price.

Interest
Leasehold
Term
30 yrs + renewals
Constraint
Renewal clause

Newer instrument

Right over Property

Sap-Ing-Sith: a registrable right created under Thailand's Right over Property Act, running up to thirty years, transferable and capable of securing a loan — closer to ownership than a lease in several practical respects. Still uncommon, so both sides need proper counsel.

Interest
Registrable right
Term
Up to 30 yrs
Constraint
Few precedents

Stewardship

The reason owners stay
is not the purchase.

It is that the residence keeps working while they are in Shanghai, Tokyo or Singapore — tenanted, maintained, insured, and accounted for, without a single flight.

  • ·Tenant sourcing, screening and lease renewal
  • ·Repairs and contractor supervision, quoted before work begins
  • ·Common-area fees, utilities and juristic-office correspondence
  • ·A monthly statement, with photographs when anything changes
  • ·Resale conducted when you decide to exit

Process

Five stages, and you may stop at any of them.

Nothing is charged until a transfer completes. Counsel before that point is ours to give.

  1. 01

    Brief

    A conversation in your language about budget, purpose, and how often you will actually be here.

  2. 02

    Shortlist

    Six to ten residences, each with its title status and quota position stated before you ask.

  3. 03

    Inspection

    In person, or a live walkthrough on video with the questions you asked us to put.

  4. 04

    Transfer

    Independent counsel, FET documentation, Land Office. Conducted remotely if you cannot travel.

  5. 05

    Tenure

    Furnishing, tenants and stewardship — or simply the keys, and nothing further.
